# Professional

I didn't feel like there was a single prominent thing in terms of research or teaching
this month, but lots of small things. Again, there was a lot of service. This accounted for
19 days of work, with a fair amount of travel in there as well.

Continuing to jointly develop a project for new master's student Zheng. Undergrads are doing well;

### Ten hours in Sydney (October 21)

I had noticed that biking seemed to help with the pinched nerve, so I rented a bicycle
and rode the Cook River Cycleway. The rental guy at the Sydney Olympic Park seemed surprised
at my plan. I guess people usually just ride around the park.

The cycleway runs, well, parallel to the Cook River, with a lot of it
lined with concrete slabs.  I have no pictures of that part of the
Cook River. I also had decided to pack light, so actually no camera at
all except for cellphone camera. Maybe I could have brought something
else. Oh well. Also, because the cycleway was all in parks, it was
calm, but did not feel urban at all. Tradeoffs.

If I think about the piste cyclable Notre-Dame in Montreal, which I
did a lot when I was a kid and visiting my grandma, it was definitely
much more urban, even if most of it was surrounded by greenery and
trees. The not ideal part was that there are trucks on Notre-Dame, and
a lot of street crossings of busy streets. Thus, [a
complaint](https://collectifeme.org/la-piste-cyclable-notre-dame-dangereuse-et-non-conviviale/).
It is definitely a better practice to not have street crossings, but
that would be impossible for a cycle track parallel to Notre-Dame, unless
it was south of it, where the heavy industry and port are.

## Round the Mountain attempt, October 22-27

Well, I got another helicopter ride, unplanned, following a knee dislocation (not mine), 15
minutes beyond Rangipo Hut. The DOC times line up decently well with our walking speed,
but it's not clear to me why they are so slow. The terrain doesn't *feel* difficult, but
then the time is slower than the elevation and distance suggest they should be.

We then lined up alternate accommodation, since we weren't going to make it to the huts,
mostly in Ohakune. Very much shoulder season, after the lifts are closed, but still not summer yet.
I think mountain biking would have been possible.

The Okahune Carrotland Adventure Park, with the big carrot (7.5m) as
the main drawing card, is surprisingly compelling, after getting an
eclair, as I usually do whenever I pass through Ohakune. There are
other large giant veges (parsnips, potatoes, brussels sprouts), as
well as an endurance course, pirate ship, flying fox, and a carrot
"space rocket". I do like the brussels sprouts I can get in Ohakune.

We then proceeded to Whanganui Accident & Medical next to the hospital (but it's actually just a clinic) to
get an X-ray. Had a bit of excitement coming back, with the fuel range showing as little as 4km. I attempted
to take the Durie Hill Elevator, but apparently it's temporarily closed.

We had a reservation for a cabin at the Whakapapa Holiday Park. We were supposed to walk
there, but it's also possible to drive there, so we did that. The cabin was huge! I think there were
6 beds? Holiday park cabins are often a great deal in New Zealand. Dinner at the Skotel was kind of average.

On the last day, I took a walk out to Rangipo to try to find my lost
point-and-shoot camera.  I made pretty good time back to the hut (2h
each way, vs 2h45 the other day), but did not find the camera. Can't
win em all. It started to get quite windy in the afternoon, as
predicted by the MetService weather forecast.  Apparently the rangers
were saying that it would get windy earlier, but the weather forecast
was correct.

The 4h drive back to Wellington is a bit long. Wouldn't want to do it every weekend.

## Food

Round the Mountain attempt: instead of trail food, we had:
* [ZenEats, Levin](https://www.facebook.com/ZenArezs/): they have upgraded from a food truck to a restaurant; good for them, and great food. I note that they now call it cheese fries instead of poutine on their menu. It's not poutine.
* [Crystal Bar Tearooms and Bakery, Taihape](https://www.facebook.com/p/Crystal-bar-Taihape-61593103032519/): standard bakery, with croissants actually a good lunch.
* [Hungry Rush, Ohakune](https://hungryrush.co.nz/): hard to mess up a burger; still, they made a proper burger.
* Kebabholik, Whanganui: also a standard mixed plate on rice.
* [Banh Mi Craft, Whanganui](https://www.facebook.com/banhmicraft/): good to see a source of banh mi in Whanganui. Above average.
* [Flavour Farm, Ohakune](https://www.facebook.com/theflavourfarm.co.nz/): generally good, could have used more salt.
* [Skotel, Whakapapa](https://www.skotel.co.nz/): great view, average pizza
* [Aamod, Bulls](https://www.aamodindian.co.nz/): Bulls is a super common stop on the way to the mountain; the mutton hyderabadi was really tasty.
* [Elemental Eats, Porirua](https://www.elementaleats.co.nz/): another Chocotoberfest dessert; worthy pie; walking from the parking to the restaurant is a hassle, in particular crossing a busy road.
