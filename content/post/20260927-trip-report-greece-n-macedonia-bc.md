This year's vacation was to Greece (for another round on Olympus),
North Macedonia (because it is right next door), and BC (because it is
on the way). We stopped in Sydney, Australia on the way there, and Sidney, BC on the way back.

# Greece, August 14-18

* [Photos: WLG-SYD-YVR-YYZ-ATH, August 14](https://gallery.patricklam.ca/index.php?/category/2358)

The flights were a bit of a doozy: Wellington to Sydney to Vancouver
to Toronto to Athens. I calculated 42 hours from Wellington to central
Athens, with longest connection 2.5 hours in YYZ. It was two tickets
with a split and 110 minute connection in Vancouver. Fortunately we
didn't have a misconnect.

The first order of business was to head to Olympus, where Mario,
Nikos, and I planned to get up to the Muses Plateau via the [Naoum
Ridge](https://www.routes.gr/?Page=en/Climbing/Routes/RidgeOfNaoum). MP would join us at the Apostolidis Refuge. Unfortunately,
there was a bit of a white-out going on at times, and we weren't
confident in routefinding in these conditions, so we went back down to
the car and then back up via Xerolakki, arriving at the refuge just
after sunset.

After a night at Apostolidis, MP and I summited Mytikas and hence
Olympus. A bit scrambly via Louki, but nothing we couldn't handle. I
wonder about the roped guided parties. I guess the guide is pretty
confident that he can catch a fall, but yikes.

We then all descended, in the end, via the Barbalas
Ridge. Routefinding also not trivial, and at times, a class 3
scramble. The last part of the class-2 trail was not at all obvious
(maybe it no longer exists) and we took the long way around, including
road walking, but we got there eventually.

After a brilliant dinner at the inn in Petra (so much food), we slept
at the inn again and then went to a legit Byzantine castle nearby,
Platamon. MP misses castles. There aren't any real castles in NZ.

We had about a day in Thessaloniki before continuing to North
Macedonia. This included a bird tour by Michael of SKG Bus, a pretty
interesting guy. He's not really a birder but can hire one if you
ask. Anyway, we did see a bunch of birds. Would have been good to have
a 100-400 lens but mine is too heavy to bring all around North
Macedonia, so I just had my very average 55-210.

Thessaloniki has some pedestrian areas but also a lot of car-dominated

a car-dominated street. There is also the White Tower, which we
admired from the outside (ascending it at the end of our trip), and the waterfront area.

# North Macedonia, August 18-24

So, the question was, where should we go after Olympus? I like the
Balkans, and the obvious choice for geographical reasons was North
Macedonia, just a few hours from Thessaloniki. I had also realized
that one could fly back from Thessaloniki to Canada (via Munich), since my friends
had done it earlier in the summer.

It's not really possible to take the train from Greece to North
Macedonia. One could connect from the bus to the train, but it's
unclear that this is a win. We took the bus from Thessaloniki to
Skopje. It's a 4h bus, including a lengthy wait in traffic at the
North Macedonia border crossing. After the crossing, there was a
chance to visit the depanneur over the border in North Macedonia, and we chatted with
some Americans who were touring the Balkans, as we waited for the bus
to leave the depanneur.

## Skopje, August 18-19

I really liked Skopje; the large bazaar area is fully pedestrianized
and lively. There was also a weird thing with statues and fountains
that were put up in 2012 or something for an enormous amount of
cash. Walking from the bus station to the bazaar was quite suburban
and car-centric, but there were still large trees and not so much power
centres. Skopje also had many public water points, which is
important with the heat. And then there were the statues of [Skopje 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skopje_2014),
which were kind of bonkers.

Skopje also has a prominent castle. We visited it. The castle walls
have been rebuilt. Inside the castle walls, there are some restored
buildings, but mostly it's just an area inside the walls with trees
and grass. There were some people doing a garbage cleanup (though in
general there was quite a bit of litter).

Shopska salad (tomatoes, cheese) was a food highlight from North
Macedonia. Also qofte-like foods, as one would expect from the region.

## Korab (and Tetovo), August 20

I got sidetracked writing the Korab report. It's written, but we'll
post it after this post. I haven't put the pictures into the report
yet.  Here are highlights from the report.

* [Photos: Korab ascent, August 20](https://gallery.patricklam.ca/index.php?/category/2357)

After Olympus, I thought that Mount Korab would be a good destination.
It is the highpoint of both North Macedonia and Albania, at 2764m. It
can be reached from either Albania (via Radomir) or North Macedonia
(via Strezimir), but since we were visting North Macedonia, we'd go
via Strezimir.

Following recommendations on the Internet, I rented a 4wd. I found one
at Relax Rent-a-Car in Skopje.

We stopped at the Colourful Mosque in Tetovo, which was on the way.
Parking in Tetovo was chaotic but we managed. Had to wait till the end of
prayer and then visited the inside of the mosque. It has lots of painted
frescoes and is somewhat modest as an attraction.

We also got a guard dog barking outside for much of the night, but
I guess that just comes with the territory. The hotel also comes with
ponies and cats.

If we hadn't had to lug our stuff around North Macedonia, I understand
that camping is also a possibility, and gets you an hour closer
to the trailhead on hike day.

We started at 7am. The first part is still on a pretty big track (4x4s
could do it for sure) and in the forest. Soon enough, though, the
forest ends and we'd be above treeline for almost all of the walk. We
passed by some run-down buildings and a stream just after treeline.

I'm not quite sure I would call the walk up relentless, but there was
quite little relenting in the climbing up, except for a long traverse
in the middle. I looked at the traverse and hoped I wouldn't have to
go back down into the valley. Fortunately, it was pretty much a true
traverse.

The trail is well maintained, with no scrambling required. Not quite a
sidewalk, but definitely much easier than the Barbalas Ridge we'd done
a few days ago. It's easy, but 16km round-trip with 1300m elevation
gain isn't completely easy. There was also an indicated winter route which
obviously had less avalanche exposure (since it went on top of
the ridge); probably the terrain was rougher or it was otherwise
more difficult than the summer route.

We arrived at the summit at 11:30, so about 4h30 after starting.
There were 17 people on the summit when we were there, with all but 4
of us coming from Radomir on the Albanian side. We had a leisurely
50-minute lunch, eating one huge sandwich and assorted snacks, and
chatted with a group of 7 from the UK who were doing a walking
holiday.

The descent was pretty much the same as the ascent, except that
we were like, "gee, that was a lot of climbing to get up there."
I took a few more photo stops for birds. I wouldn't have minded having
my 100-400 lens, but that's an extra 700g for a small number of bird shots.
One can spot birds atop rocks and posts sometimes.

We did drink all of our 3L of water. I could have treated some water
from the stream, but by that time, we were close enough that we just went
back to the car.

We also crossed a group of 21 which was walking up pretty late in the day:
like 1pm or later. Sounds like an opportunity to see the sunset on the
way down to me. At the parking lot, there were quite a few more cars
than when we had left in the morning (there was only us and the other group).

Our moving time, excluding lunch, was 7h30, with 3h for the descent.

We drove back to Skopje, got a place near the bus station, and I had a
bit of an adventure returning the car; see the full TR for details.

Soon after arriving at the apartment, we decided that Bella Pizza
would be a good dinner, so I fetched something pide-like, with quite
hot bell peppers, for 480MKD.  The staff said the picture online
wasn't quite right, but it was cheese and bacon. Yes, I like cheese
and bacon. It would have been better if I had remembered which floor the
apartment was on, but I got there eventually, and we enjoyed the pide.
Pizza Bella deserves its 4.9* on Google Maps. And, as I was doing all the
faffing, MP was doing our laundry, so we'd have more clean laundry.
Good deal.

Overall, a successful hike, two high points, and a bunch of life
maintenance.  What more could one want?

## Ohrid, August 21-22

Ohrid is a resort town, with mostly local tourism (e.g. people from
Turikye, which is maybe a 9 hour drive away). I guess the main point
is that it is on Lake Ohrid, a deep lake which also borders
Albania. We had a day in Ohrid, leaving the next day for Bitola.

Apart from the lake (which has a bustling lakeshore scene and some
modest beaches in town; apparently better ones further out),
there is a bazaar, many Orthodox churches, and Tsar Samuel's Fortress
(another castle). Once again, the bazaar is quite human-friendly,
and there are indeed lots of humans on foot.

I could imagine going back for a day on the lake or a bike ride, but
it really was kind of overrun with tourists. It was less hot than
Skopje though.

Just before leaving, we got a ride on the semi submarine. In principle
you can look below the surface of the lake, but there's not a lot of
visibility. One might see eels and fish. It was nice to be on the top side,
though; basically, a short lake cruise. Could do a longer cruise.

## Bitola, August 22-24

Luxury! Two nights in the same place! But we had to get there first.
A short 1h bus ride took us from Ohrid to Bitola, where we checked in at
Robevski Luxury Rooms. We definitely had much more space than in Ohrid;
there was a living room separate from the bedroom. The decor was... fancy.

We visited Heraclea Lyncestis, with more ruins, including an amphitheatre,
but also lots of well-preserved mosaics. There is a page on the Internet
where someone is [really excited about the mosaics](https://helenmilesmosaics.org/ancient-mosaics/heraclea-lyncestis-mosaics/). We appreciated them,
but we're not experts. You could definitely make out octopuses, phoenixes,
doves, etc.

Also on the agenda was the Pelister Eyes. This did not require a car
rental; we had gotten a taxi driver from the bus station upon arriving
in Bitola to save a 2km walk, and he drove us to the hike (and also to
Florina in Greece). Probably we could've gotten a ride back to town
from the hike.

The Pelister Eyes was our second hike in a North Macedonian national
park; this was the Pelister National Park. The nice thing is that even
if it's pretty hot at sea level, it's not hot in the mountains.  The
temperature was great. It was still a 19km hike with 1100m of
elevation gain, so not quite nothing, but again, well-maintained
trails (with litter).

The first eye (lake) (Golemo Ezero) has a hut next to it. It was pretty popular that day,
with dozens of people there. I'm not sure where they came from. We didn't
see them on the trails.

There were also horses. I think the deal is that the horses bring stuff
up to the hut and then are left to graze. There was one horse which was much
more interested in stealing human food.

We reached the second eye (Malo Ezero), about 40 minutes further, and then walked
back to the trailhead.

Also in Bitola I finally managed to get a Euro power cable. Thessaloniki, a much larger
city, was unable to provide. I also have this cable at my house, of course,
but wasn't there.

# British Columbia, August 25-27

After flying across the Atlantic and North America, we got to
Vancouver, where we would do yet another hike. Some of the hikes are
at the end of rough Forest Service Roads and may require high
clearance. Fortunately, [Elfin Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfin_Lakes) was not one of them. Super scenic
and easy hike. High effort to reward ratio; it was still 20km and 710m
of elevation gain, but really it was a first-class trail.  We saw many
people and 4 bears.

MP had read on the Internet that Victoria was the Canadian city most
like Wellington; I was skeptical. But we went there for a day and a
night, taking the ferry from Vancouver in the morning.

I find Victoria pretty inaccessible (it's on an island), but I guess it is less far than
New Zealand. Reports from the locals were that it is kind of a sleepy
place. Lots of tourists. We had lots of sushi in BC, did not have
poutine (thought about it), and did have tapas. Water taxis are
cute. The geography means that if the orcas swim into the bay, then
you can wait and see them coming out.  So we saw 7 orcas.

I feel like tourism in North America is pretty expensive these days,
and didn't really like the $260/night cost of our hotel in Victoria.
It was a great room, though. But there aren't really cheaper options there.
NZ seems cheaper most of the time.
