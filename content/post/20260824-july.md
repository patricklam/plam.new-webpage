## Collegiality/Service

It's been interesting to serve as PC co-chair even if it's a lot of work. As a PC member you see a bit about how the sausage is made in terms of accepting papers, but as a PC co-chair, you see the trends over all the papers (except for your conflicts) and get a better idea of what papers may or may not get in.

Consistent with the SCAM charter, I reiterated that there was no target accept rate, though of course I computed the eventual accept rate and was relieved that it was in line.

The other interesting factoid about submissions is that they seem to be up across the board this year, likely because of LLM assistance in writing tools and words. I kind of wonder if 10 years from now we're just going to say "you know that AI bubble, we don't trust any papers produced during it." SCAM got twice as many submissions as last year, but it's also in Europe and not New Zealand. POPL got a huge increase in submissions as well and I got a paper to review following their reserve reviewer policy.

It was a pleasure to work with Carolin Brandt, my PC co-chair.

Aside from that, there was a bit of FAUW governance work and a meeting/demo with
the ECE FYDP group.

## Auckland Open, July 24&ndash;27

Next up, the Auckland International Open judo tournament.  Gotta go on
the Friday: can't fly early enough on Saturday (and that would be
tiring). I decided to go early on Friday and to return on the Monday
evening, with a cheap airbnb 2km from the tournament site (St
Helliers). The airbnb was actually kind of like a cabin on the
property, though I had to walk around and inside to use the bathroom.

One surprising thing about central Auckland was the lack of people on a Friday morning
at rush hour. Toronto is packed with people, especially at Union Station. The train to Waiatemata, and the CBD itself, really not.
Not a long weekend, just a normal winter workday. Where is everyone?

I met up with Rob O'Callahan for lunch on Friday. This was the first time
I'd been to a Google office since 2019. Even if things may have changed at
Google, the lunch seemed to be what I remembered from the good old days.

Otherwise, I did more SCAM work in Auckland (e.g. at the library) before
proceeding to weigh-in and then choban kebap for dinner at Darya near the venue,
which was good. Had some other tasty food in Auckland as well.

Had a 5 man division in Veteran Mens 40-49 -66kg; two wins got me the
gold medal, though I needed to go to golden score in the
final. Category size not bad, though I didn't fight any black
belts. Other than that, I refereed the rest of the tournament.

Since the tournament ended at 2-ish, I had the chance to go climbing
at Extreme Edge. It turns out that the bouldering problems there are tough, though
I managed to climb a few. Three of the boulderers were also from Wellington, either
having moved to Auckland from Wellington, or on vacation.

What to do on the Monday? Good weather, so I had a late start, got
breakfast at a nearby deli and did travel planning and a bit of
analysis for SCAM, then walked through Auckland and to the Auckland
Museum, which is worth a visit. They have at least two taxidermied
kākāpo.

## Books

* Peter Hurley. _The Headshot: The Secrets to Creating Amazing Headshot Portraits_.

MP complains that I'm not very good at consistently taking good
pictures of her.  I tried to read this book to learn more. The author
has an interesting life story (Olympic sailor to model/actor to
photographer) and explains his headshot process clearly, so I think I
did learn a few things.

Portraits are quite different from landscapes, birds, and street
photography, which are the main forms of photography that I do. The
amount of control in a studio is way higher. The bird is not going to
pose for you! At best, you figure out what the bird is going to do,
and you pre-position yourself to take the picture of the bird.

The fundamental problem, though, is that I can't really look at a
headshot and say "this is good" and "this is bad". I'm a bit face
blind. Doesn't help. For instance, at one point, he has two pictures
of a subject, and says "this side is the good side". I really can't
tell.

I'm not 100% hopeless though. He references this video of his:

* [How to get a better jawline in photos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qe3oJnFtA_k)

and I do see what he's talking about. So that is one tip that I can use.
He's also the source of the line "always be squinching". He definitely squinches
in that video, and I think it's a bit excessive.

More generally, I'd say that a key idea of the book is to find ways to
develop rapport with the subject and to get them to look their best
for you. But not necessarily in a straightforward way: he says that he
uses misdirection to get people away from thinking that they are in
front of a camera (and thus looking weird).

Anyway, yes, books are a good source of curated information by an
author. Better at being a body of work than a bunch of blog posts. He
uses a lot of pictures to illustrate his point (as he should); not
being able to fully understand them is a me problem.

## Food

Wellington:
* [Peckish Jr](https://www.instagram.com/peckish.jr/): it's been there for
a while but I just noticed it and learned that it does Vietnamese food;
rivals Fisherman's Plate for pho and has banh mi, unlike FP.

* [The Larder](https://www.thelarder.co.nz/): had a good burger as I was out for brunch with friends.

Blenheim/Kaikōura:
* [Saint Clair Family Estate](https://saintclair.co.nz/pages/vineyard-kitchen): absolutely delicious gnocchi; also tons of people on bicycle tours.
* [Tipsy Foodie's](https://brand.site/tipsyfoodies): they do other things, but it was a fairly standard meat on chips that we shared.
* [Asian Taste](https://evendo.com/locations/new-zealand/kaikoura/restaurant/asian-taste): good food truck with, indeed, Asian food. Haven't had Singapore noodles for a long time, but this is what I expected.
* [Strawberry Tree](https://www.strawberrytreekaikoura.co.nz/): chatted with the friendly French-from-France working holidayers who were on staff twice; portions large; fire was nice; food was OK.
* [Black Rabbit Pizza](https://www.blackrabbitpizza.co.nz/): more jalapeno, this time in a toasty, which was just what I wanted for breakfast.
* [Kaikōura Seafood BBQ](https://www.kaikoura.co.nz/listing/kaikoura-seafood-bbq): we'd been here before but maybe we ordered better this time or the staff were more happy to see us; in any case, this place is famous, and this time, the scallops were worthy.

Auckland:
* [Darya Afghan Cuisine](https://darya.co.nz/): great chopan kebab near the tournament venue.
* [Acacia](https://www.acaciarestaurant.co.nz/): Hotel restaurant, but we benefited from a 20% off frequent-hotel discount from a colleague, so the burger was actually reasonably priced; definitely wouldn't do the buffet.
* [Nikki's Kitchen](https://www.facebook.com/p/Nikkis-Kitchen-61555624177934/): excellent source of pies in the morning; in Canada I end up often getting McDonald's for breakfast before refereeing, but I prefer pies for sure.
* [Lumbini Indian & Nepalese Cuisine](https://lumbini.co.nz/): keeping with the food-next-to-climbing-gym theme, the goat curry was pretty good.
* [Browns St Heliers](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100063671468222): I'd guess that this place is run by Vietnamese immigrants, and I enjoyed my benedict-on-rosti (even if that's not quite correct).
* [Ohmiya](https://www.ohmiya.co.nz/): good-sushi-for-New-Zealand, with a reasonable bento box.

Levin:
* [Thai Country Chef](https://www.thaicountrychef.co.nz/menu): kind of average.
